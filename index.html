KCL Refund Rights: Consumer Law, Evidence and Student Action

KCL students may have paid full tuition for a course that was materially different from what was advertised or contracted for. If your practical teaching, laboratory access, supervised experimental work or specialist in-person provision was removed or substantially reduced, you may have a consumer-law argument for a refund or price reduction.

This site explains the argument, the evidence to gather, and the complaint route I am using after KCL declined to engage further and directed me to the OIA.

The issue is not only that COVID happened; it is whether students paid for a represented educational service and received something materially different without proper equivalent provision, refund assessment, record search or remedy.

Why a refund may be justified

The basis is contract, consumer law and loss of educational value

1. Contractual basis

Students paid tuition fees and KCL agreed to provide the educational service described in course materials, module information, pre-contract communications and student terms.

2. Binding course information

If course materials described a lab-based degree with practical teaching, specialist equipment and supervised experiments, students may argue they relied on those representations.

3. Material difference

A full-fee charge may be challenged where high-value practical or laboratory elements were removed or substantially reduced and the delivered course was materially different.

4. Reasonable alternatives

COVID restrictions were real, but they do not automatically answer whether reasonable equivalent provision, proper assessment or remedies were offered.

5. Price reduction

If the service was not performed with reasonable care and skill, or did not match binding information, students may argue for an appropriate refund or price reduction.

6. Record-search fairness

If KCL says there is "no record", ask exactly which systems, dates, search terms, student numbers, archived email and finance/refund records were checked.

Exact legal basis

The consumer-law argument in detail

Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 49: reasonable care and skill

A contract to supply a service includes a term that the trader must perform the service with reasonable care and skill. In a university context, the argument is not simply whether COVID was outside KCL's control. The question is whether the course was delivered, adapted, explained and remedied with reasonable care, especially where practical/laboratory elements were central to the course.

Read section 49

Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 50: information about the service can be binding

Information said or written about the trader or service can be binding if the consumer took it into account. This is central where students relied on KCL descriptions of laboratory work, practical teaching, specialist equipment, supervised experiments and in-person scientific training.

Read section 50

Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 56: price reduction

In appropriate cases, the consumer remedy may include a price reduction. Students do not need to frame this as an automatic full refund. The stronger route is to explain the lost educational value, why it was material, and what proportion of fees should fairly be refunded.

Read section 56

Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 62: fair terms and notices

Unfair terms or notices are not binding. KCL can have policies and deadlines, but students can argue that those policies must be applied fairly, transparently and with proper reasons, especially where KCL controls the relevant records.

Read section 62

CMA higher education consumer guidance

The CMA has guidance for higher education providers on consumer law, including information provision, fair terms and complaint handling. This supports the argument that universities must treat course representations and refund complaints seriously.

Read CMA guidance

Claim guide

How to claim without a lawyer

  1. Identify your course promise. Save prospectus pages, module handbooks, lab/practical descriptions, offer emails and screenshots.
  2. Identify what changed. List missed labs, practical sessions, specialist equipment, supervised experiments, placements, tutorials and technical support failures.
  3. Calculate value lost. Estimate the proportion of your course that was practical/lab-based, the duration affected and the remedy you seek.
  4. Search your own records. Search for refund, COVID refund, tuition fee, complaint, appeal, credit control, funding, reimbursement and bank details.
  5. Ask KCL for records. Send a Subject Access Request for archived email, finance/refund-processing, Student Funding, Credit Control and complaints records.
  6. Make or escalate a formal complaint. Frame it as consumer/service delivery/refund handling/records issue, not merely academic dissatisfaction.
  7. Request detailed reasons if rejected. Ask which systems, dates, search terms, identifiers and departments were checked.
  8. Use the OIA route after a Completion of Procedures letter. Focus on procedural unfairness, inadequate reasons and record-search inadequacy.
  9. Keep it calm and evidenced. Use “KCL has not explained” rather than unsupported accusations.

Copyable templates

Use these paragraphs as a starting point

Subject Access Request

Please treat this as a Subject Access Request under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. Please provide all personal data relating to my tuition fees, COVID-19 refund, complaint, appeal, finance/refund processing, Student Funding, Credit Control, Student Conduct & Appeals correspondence, archived/deactivated student email records, internal notes, case logs and search logs. Please search my name, current email, former student email, all student numbers and the period from 1 January 2020 to the present.

Complaint paragraph

My complaint is not only about historical COVID disruption. It concerns the material difference between the laboratory-based programme represented to me and the service delivered, and the provider's handling of my refund/price-reduction request under consumer-law principles.

Record-search request

Please identify every system searched, date range used, search terms used, student identifiers checked, departments contacted, and whether archived student email, finance/refund-processing records, Student Funding, Credit Control and Student Conduct & Appeals records were searched.

OIA paragraph

KCL's decision was procedurally unfair and inadequately reasoned because it relied on absence of evidence without explaining the searches of KCL-controlled records that I could no longer access as a former student.

Evidence checklist

Build your evidence bundle

  • Course advertisements and prospectus pages
  • Module descriptions and learning outcomes
  • Lab/practical timetable evidence
  • Emails with staff about disrupted provision
  • Screenshots of online replacements or missing labs
  • Tuition fee invoices/payment records
  • Complaint/refund emails
  • Former student email access issue
  • KCL responses saying no record
  • Student number(s) and former email address
  • Evidence of practical/lab dependency of course

External route

Using the OIA after KCL closes the internal process

If you receive a Completion of Procedures letter, consider the OIA route within the relevant deadline. The OIA will usually focus on whether the provider handled the complaint fairly and reasonably, whether it gave adequate reasons, and whether the outcome was reasonable in the circumstances.

For this kind of complaint, focus on mischaracterisation, inadequate record-search explanation, reliance on absence of evidence in KCL-controlled systems, and failure to consider the consumer/refund context.

FAQ

Questions students usually ask

Is this guaranteed?

No. Entitlement depends on the individual course, evidence, representations, loss of value, deadlines and how KCL handled the complaint.

Do I need a lawyer?

Not necessarily for an internal complaint or OIA complaint. This site focuses on self-advocacy, argumentation and evidence.

What if KCL says COVID was outside its control?

That is relevant, but not the end of the analysis. Ask what reasonable alternatives, refund assessments and remedies were provided.

What if KCL says full refunds were not generally offered?

Ask whether your individual case involved a specific representation, approval, processing record or material loss of value.

What if KCL says it has no records?

Ask for the search methodology and submit a SAR for archived email, finance/refund and complaint records.

Should I publicly accuse KCL of hiding records?

No. Keep public statements evidence-based and neutral unless you have proof. Say what KCL has or has not explained.
